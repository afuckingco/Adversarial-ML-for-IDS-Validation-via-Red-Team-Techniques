Dear Editors,

I am submitting the manuscript entitled "Adaptive Evasion Traffic Generator (AETG): A Framework for Adaptive IDS Validation via Red-Team Techniques" for consideration for publication in Computers & Security.

This manuscript presents a novel framework, AETG, that unifies (1) protocol-compliant traffic generation, (2) TLS fingerprint (JA3/JA4) randomization, (3) adaptive evasion via a contextual Multi-Armed Bandit (LinUCB) with real-time IDS feedback through Redis, and (4) an Evasion Stealth Metric (ESM) based on Jensen-Shannon Divergence.

The framework was validated in two distinct settings:
1. A calibration environment (mock HTTP server) achieving 96.8% evasion rate.
2. A mini-SOC deployment (Suricata with 52,415 Emerging Threats rules, Redis alert pipeline) where an XGBoost-based ML detector achieved recall = 1.0 on 200 adversarial C2 beacon flows.

The contributions of this work are:
- An end-to-end framework for protocol-aware adversarial traffic generation.
- LinUCB-based adaptive evasion with real-time IDS feedback.
- A quantitative stealth metric (ESM) based on Jensen-Shannon Divergence.
- Validation against a production-grade mini-SOC deployment.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission.

We believe this work is particularly well-suited for Computers & Security because it addresses the intersection of adversarial machine learning and network security, a topic of increasing importance to both researchers and practitioners.

I would be happy to address any questions or provide additional information.
